ABSTRACT (521 words)

Scientific conferences provide opportunities for researchers to exchange findings, compare methods, and identify questions that benefit from collaboration. However, participants may encounter practical barriers when discovering relevant work or initiating conversations across disciplines. This illustrative study explores how a structured digital submission process could support clearer communication before a scientific meeting. The objective is to describe a practical framework for collecting research summaries while maintaining accessible forms, consistent author information, and transparent communication. The example is fictional and demonstrates abstract presentation only; it does not report findings from an actual investigation or support clinical decisions.

The proposed framework combines an online abstract form with a review stage and a concise acknowledgement message. Authors enter their contact information, institutional affiliation, research title, scientific track, and preferred presentation format. Additional contributors can be listed in publication order, with their affiliations recorded separately. Abstract text is reviewed against a defined word range, while an optional document attachment accommodates established institutional workflows. The interface makes required information visible and explains the relationship between abstract submission, scientific assessment, and conference registration. Research teams should adapt these elements to their own governance and participation requirements.

An evaluation plan would examine usability, completeness of submissions, and the clarity of communication received by authors. Participants could be asked to complete representative tasks using the form and then describe any points of uncertainty. Observations would focus on finding instructions, entering international contact details, adding collaborators, selecting a presentation preference, and reviewing the final content. The assessment would also consider keyboard navigation, readable labels, error recovery, and use on smaller screens. Any evaluation involving people should follow applicable institutional requirements, with informed participation and appropriate protection of personal information throughout the process.

The expected contribution is a reusable approach to submission design rather than a claim about measured performance. Clear instructions may help authors understand the required content before beginning, while an explicit review step may provide an opportunity to correct omissions. A simple acknowledgement can communicate receipt without implying acceptance or promising a particular presentation format. The final decision remains the responsibility of the scientific committee. Actual improvements in completion rates, processing effort, or author satisfaction would need to be established through a suitable evaluation rather than inferred from the appearance of the interface alone.

Several limitations require attention before implementation. An online form cannot independently establish research quality, verify authorship, or determine whether ethical requirements have been met. Organisers must define review responsibilities, submission deadlines, access permissions, retention periods, and the conditions for any publication. Secure storage, validated file handling, reliable email delivery, and appropriate verification mechanisms are also necessary. These operational considerations should be agreed before collecting live submissions. The proposed framework offers a starting point for discussion and iterative testing, while leaving scientific judgement and conference policy with the responsible organisers and reviewers. Clear communication should extend to every stage of the author journey. Organisers can explain when a submission has been received, how contributors will be contacted, and where updates will appear. Consistent wording across the form and acknowledgement email can reduce uncertainty while giving researchers a straightforward record of their interaction with the conference team.
